The isomorphism to Z₃, and its limits

The most-cited idea in the fleet, stated carefully — including the part where the analogy runs out.

Gauss's law, briefly

Electric flux through a closed surface equals the enclosed charge. The tool that makes it work is divergence: a local quantity whose integral over a region is the flux through its boundary, independent of the surface chosen.

Where it stops

The load-bearing caution

Z₃ is abelian. Agent interaction is not. The order of two bottles into a cell generally matters, and Z₃ addition does not. Any argument that depends on the substrate being Z₃ is an argument about a commutative system the substrate is not. The conservation check itself is fine — it is a single local sum — but "therefore the whole system is a Z₃ gauge field" does not follow.

What it does buy

An invariant you can check in O(1) per interaction, with no global state. That is worth having on its own terms, and it is what the kernel actually implements. Everything beyond the local sum is interpretation, and this page marks where interpretation starts.
